---
title: Using the results responsibly
summary: How to use scores fairly and safely, and what they must never be used for.
---

A risk score is a starting point for a conversation with a household, not a verdict about it. These principles apply wherever the tool is used.

## A ranking, not a decision

Use scores to decide the **order** of visits when there are more households than a team can reach. Never use a score on its own to grant, refuse or end support. Eligibility must be decided by people, through a proper assessment and the programme's own rules.

## Always confirm with a visit

The model gets roughly one household in five wrong. Every flagged household should be visited and assessed, and a household that was not flagged but is clearly in need should never be turned away because of its score.

## Be careful with comparisons between areas

Because the survey area has a large effect, scores are most useful for comparing households **within** the same area. Differences between areas reflect patterns in 2018–2022 and should be checked against current information before they shape where resources go.

## Protect household privacy

The interview does not ask for names, ID numbers or locations, and none should be added. The scoring service calculates a score and does not save the answers. When uploading a list, remove names and identifiers first and keep the results as securely as any other household records.

## Questions to the AI assistant

The knowledge centre's question box sends your question to an external AI service (Groq) to write an answer from these articles. Do not include names, personal details or information about specific households in a question.
